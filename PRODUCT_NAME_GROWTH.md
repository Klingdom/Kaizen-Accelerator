# PRODUCT_NAME_GROWTH.md
# Naming Recommendation — Growth Strategist

---

## 1. Brand Fragmentation Assessment

**CadencePlan** (app.html — the live product)
Carries discipline and sequencing. "Cadence" is used by sales teams (Outreach, Salesloft cadences), so it has SaaS recognition — but also baggage. "Plan" is generic and weak. Together: forgettable compound noun. No emotional pull. Hard to verb. No one says "I cadenceplan my mornings." It does not communicate improvement, only scheduling.

**Kaizen Accelerator** (marketing site — 7 pages, the dominant public surface)
This is the brand with the most surface area and the clearest market positioning ("fix one critical process in 30 days, $20K fixed fee"). "Kaizen" carries lean/ops heritage — respected by practitioners, opaque to generalist managers. "Accelerator" is overloaded (startup programs, Y Combinator, etc.) and mildly misleading when applied to a 30-day consulting engagement. The combo reads as a consulting offer, not a software product. That may be intentional for the services side — but it creates a split-product problem when the app (CadencePlan) is a distinct SaaS surface.

**BAM-X Today / BAM-X Kaizen OS** (design files, package.json)
Internal shorthand that leaked. "BAM-X" is meaningless to any external person, sounds like an energy drink or action-camera brand, and creates zero positioning value. It should be retired entirely from any user-facing surface immediately regardless of what name is chosen.

**What is at stake**
The product is operating as two brands on the same codebase: a consulting practice (Kaizen Accelerator) and a daily scheduling SaaS (CadencePlan). Picking one name means deciding whether the software product and the consulting offer are sold as the same thing or as separate products. That is the upstream strategic call. This document assumes the goal is a unified SaaS product name that can serve both surfaces — with the consulting offer becoming a tier or service layer under the same brand.

## 2. Naming Criteria

1. **Memorable in conversation.** "I use ___ every morning" should feel natural.
2. **Communicates outcome within 2 seconds.** A stranger should grasp the general territory (planning, improvement, or daily discipline) without a footnote.
3. **Differentiated from Sunsama, Motion, and Notion Calendar.** Those own "calm," "AI-scheduled," and "calendar as database." The name must signal something they do not: structured improvement discipline, not just task capture.
4. **Works as a verb or possessive.** "I ___ my week" or "my ___ plan" builds habit-language.
5. **Trademark-defensible.** Not a generic dictionary word in the exact category of productivity software.
6. **Pronounceable globally.** No clusters of consonants or sounds that collapse in Japanese, Spanish, German, or French — especially given the Kaizen heritage, which has a Japanese-speaking practitioner community.
7. **Not methodology-locked.** A name tied exclusively to DMAIC or Kaizen will alienate OKR practitioners and general managers. The name must be broad enough to accommodate multiple improvement frameworks while still implying structured discipline.
8. **Scalable from SaaS to professional services.** The consulting tier needs to live under the same brand without sounding awkward (e.g., "Acme Enterprise" or "Acme Accelerator" should read cleanly).
9. **Domain plausibly acquirable.** Exact .com is preferred. One-word or portmanteau names have better availability than two-word phrases.
10. **Not aspirational fluff.** Words like "flow," "peak," "clarity," and "focus" are saturated in productivity. The name should carry operational weight, not wellness-brand softness.

## 3. Candidate Names (14 total, including the 4 existing)

### Existing Names

**1. CadencePlan**
Tagline: Your daily cadence, structured.
Evokes: scheduling rhythm, discipline.
Pros: clean compound, readable, not locked to Kaizen methodology.
Cons: generic, does not signal improvement, "plan" is weak, hard to verb, easily confused with sales-cadence tools.
Domain hint: cadenceplan.com — likely available.

**2. Kaizen Accelerator**
Tagline: Fix one critical process in 30 days.
Evokes: lean ops, speed, consulting engagement.
Pros: clear positioning for consulting offer, strong in ops practitioner circles.
Cons: "accelerator" is poisoned by startup programs, sounds like a service not a SaaS product, locks to Kaizen methodology, opaque to generalist ICs and managers outside lean.
Domain hint: kaizenaccelerator.com — likely available but long.

**3. BAM-X (any variant)**
Tagline: None worth writing.
Evokes: nothing coherent.
Pros: none.
Cons: meaningless, sounds like a consumer product, zero positioning value, must be retired.
Verdict: eliminated.

**4. Kaizen OS**
Tagline: The operating system for continuous improvement.
Evokes: systematic, platform-level, lean methodology.
Pros: "OS" signals depth and system-level thinking, familiar to technical buyers.
Cons: locked to Kaizen, "OS" is overused in SaaS, may create literal confusion ("is this an operating system?").
Domain hint: kaizenOS.com — likely taken or squatted.

### New Candidates

**5. Ratchet**
Tagline: Move forward. Hold the gain.
Evokes: mechanical forward progress that cannot slip backward — the physical metaphor for kaizen/CI philosophy.
Pros: one word, verbable ("I ratcheted my sprint"), unexpected in productivity, carries the non-regression idea that is central to the product's philosophy.
Cons: industrial connotation may read as rough-edged, "ratchet" has slang meaning in some English dialects (UK English: means low-class), potential global misread.
Domain hint: ratchet.io is a dev tool — ratchetapp.com or ratchethq.com likely available.

**6. Cadence** (standalone, drop "Plan")
Tagline: Structured days. Compounding results.
Evokes: rhythm, discipline, regularity.
Pros: already partially in use, cleaner than CadencePlan, one word.
Cons: heavily used by sales tools (Salesloft, Apollo), a full rebrand away from the hyphenated compound still leaves the category confusion, does not signal improvement.
Domain hint: cadence.com is taken. cadencehq.com — check availability.

**7. Fieldwork**
Tagline: The discipline layer for practitioners who build things.
Evokes: ground-level operational work, practitioner identity, not executive theater.
Pros: evokes structured daily practice without methodology lock-in, works for IC and manager alike, strong brand voice potential.
Cons: can read as ethnographic research, not immediately SaaS, verb form awkward.
Domain hint: fieldwork.com likely taken; fieldworkhq.com — check.

**8. Iterate**
Tagline: Plan today. Improve tomorrow.
Evokes: agile, continuous improvement, loops.
Pros: clean verb-as-noun, methodology-neutral, describes the product motion.
Cons: generic in software context, every agile tool implies iteration, does not differentiate.
Domain hint: iterate.com likely taken.

**9. Forma**
Tagline: Your daily form. Your sustained standard.
Evokes: structure, discipline, form as in physical practice form, Italian/Latin for shape.
Pros: short, pronounceable globally, elegant, suggests both structure and practice, feminine without being soft, non-methodology-locked.
Cons: abstract, 2-second comprehension requires a tagline assist, "form" in English also means a document (bureaucratic connotation risk).
Domain hint: forma.com likely taken; formaapp.com or formahq.com — check.

**10. Sprinta**
Tagline: Standard work, sprint cadence.
Evokes: sprint execution, velocity, structured motion.
Pros: verbable, implies urgency and rhythm.
Cons: feels agile-adjacent rather than kaizen/lean, the -a suffix feels like a startup naming pattern that has aged poorly.
Domain hint: sprinta.com — likely available.

**11. Kaizenly** (portmanteau)
Tagline: Continuous improvement, built into your day.
Evokes: kaizen heritage, daily practice.
Pros: preserves brand equity from Kaizen Accelerator marketing site.
Cons: awkward suffix, reads as an adverb ("-ly"), unlikely to be verbable.
Domain hint: kaizenlyapp.com — likely available but clunky.

**12. Cyclo**
Tagline: Plan the cycle. Close the loop.
Evokes: PDCA/DMAIC cycles, regularity, completeness.
Pros: short, pronounceable, methodology-neutral, implies the closed-loop improvement concept that differentiates from simple scheduling.
Cons: cycling as sport will dominate search results, may need "app" suffix, slightly technical.
Domain hint: cyclo.app or cyclohq.com — check.

**13. Standa**
Tagline: Your standard. Your day. Your discipline.
Evokes: standard work (the lean concept), daily commitment, personal operating standard.
Pros: directly references "standard work" — the 85-entry catalog is the product's deepest differentiator, short, globally pronounceable, not methodology-locked.
Cons: invented word requires explanation, no dictionary anchor, could read as "standard" + "-a" as a naming shortcut.
Domain hint: standaapp.com or standahq.com — likely available.

**14. Meridian**
Tagline: The high point of your day, every day.
Evokes: peak performance, a fixed reference point, navigation and orientation.
Pros: evokes precision and a fixed reference point — fits the deterministic scheduling philosophy, beautiful word, globally known.
Cons: too abstract, 2-second comprehension fails without heavy context, "meridian" reads as finance or healthcare brand (multiple co. exist), does not signal improvement or discipline.
Domain hint: meridian.com is taken; likely requires a suffix.

## 4. Top 3 Recommendations

### #1 — Standa

**Why it wins:** The product's deepest moat is its 85-entry standard-work catalog. No competitor — Motion, Sunsama, Notion Calendar — owns the "standard work" concept in SaaS. The name makes the moat visible. It does not lock to Kaizen methodology (standard work lives across lean, OKR, and agile), it is short, it is globally pronounceable, it has no dangerous existing associations, and it can verb loosely ("get your standa in order"). The consulting offer becomes "Standa Accelerator" — a clean naming structure.

Positioning headline: "Standa: the standard-work OS for practitioners who run structured days."

Voice/tone: Precise. Practitioner-first. Zero fluff. Trusts the user to be smart.

Rename scope: New domain, update all HTML titles and meta, update package.json, retire BAM-X, fold "Kaizen Accelerator" consulting as a tier named "Standa Accelerator" or "Standa 30."

---

### #2 — Ratchet

**Why it wins:** One word. Unforgettable. The mechanical metaphor — forward-only motion, gains that cannot slip — is the most honest description of what the product does philosophically. "Ratchet" is the kind of name that becomes a cult favorite among practitioners. It has no competitors in productivity SaaS. Verbable: "I ratcheted my week." The risk is the UK slang connotation and the industrial feel, which could read as rough rather than precise.

Positioning headline: "Ratchet: move forward, hold the gain. The daily discipline tool for people who run structured improvement work."

Voice/tone: Blunt, confident, practitioner-first. No softening.

Rename scope: Same as Standa. New domain (avoid ratchet.io). Ratchet App or RatchetHQ as domain path.

---

### #3 — Forma

**Why it wins:** Elegant, short, globally pronounceable, and carries dual meaning — the discipline of physical form (practice, repetition, mastery) and the concept of structure. It positions above the wellness-adjacent productivity tools without being cold. The design language of the product (Luminous Constraint, Instrument Serif, Geist) suits a name with this much aesthetic weight. The consulting offer becomes "Forma Accelerator" or "Forma Intensive."

Positioning headline: "Forma: the daily practice system for managers and IC practitioners who run standard work."

Voice/tone: Considered, confident, slightly elevated without being precious.

Rename scope: Same as above. formaapp.com or forma.so as domain path if forma.com is unavailable.

## 5. Single Top Pick

**Standa.**

The case: this product's real differentiator is not scheduling — Motion and Sunsama do scheduling. It is not Kaizen methodology — that is too narrow. It is the combination of a structured standard-work catalog plus a deterministic daily planner. No other SaaS product owns "standard work" as its identity. Standa makes that ownership visible in the name, without requiring users to know what Kaizen is, without locking to lean methodology, and without fighting the saturated vocabulary of "flow," "focus," and "cadence."

The trade-off: it is an invented word. It will not be immediately understood without a tagline. That is acceptable — Linear, Notion, and Vercel are also not immediately self-explanatory. Invented names become strong brands when the product is strong enough to fill the word with meaning. Given the depth of the standard-work catalog and the quality of the Today experience, Standa has that product behind it.

Retire: BAM-X in all forms. Retire CadencePlan. Migrate Kaizen Accelerator to a tier name (Standa Accelerator or Standa 30).

## 6. Positioning Statement

**One-sentence position:**
Standa is the daily scheduling and standard-work platform for managers and IC practitioners who run structured improvement work and need every day to compound toward a measurable outcome.

**30-word elevator pitch:**
Standa gives you a structured standard-work catalog and a deterministic daily planner. Every task has a type, every day has a pattern, and the gains do not slip.

## 7. Open Questions for Phil

1. **Are the SaaS product and the consulting offer the same brand?** The recommendation above assumes yes. If the consulting practice is intended to remain distinct and primary, "Kaizen Accelerator" should stay as-is for the consulting side and the SaaS app gets a new name. If it is one product with two tiers, pick one name and subordinate the offer beneath it.

2. **Do you want the Kaizen heritage visible in the name?** "Standa" sheds it entirely. "Ratchet" also sheds it. If your current pipeline was built on the Kaizen Accelerator brand and that term has direct recall with existing clients, shedding it has a real cost. Know what share of your qualified pipeline came in via "Kaizen Accelerator" search specifically before burning it.

3. **Which design persona do you want the name to match?** The Luminous Constraint visual system is precise and slightly cold — "Ratchet" fits that. "Standa" is neutral enough for either direction. "Forma" fits a more premium-services aesthetic. The name and the design language should agree.

4. **Is the .com domain a hard requirement?** Standa.app, Standa.so, or Standahq.com may be the realistic paths. If exact .com is a hard gate, the candidate list changes — actual domain availability requires a live check (market research scope, not this document).

5. **Are you attached to "Kaizen" as a term anywhere in your own practitioner identity?** If you publish under Kaizen, speak on Kaizen, or run a community with that word in the title, the naming decision has personal-brand implications that go beyond the product.
