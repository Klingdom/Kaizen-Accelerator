PDCA Cycle
2.0
a. Determine a target activity or process area of interest, the "theme" for the activity and a baseline current condition measurement of the activity.
B. Establish a future state target condition measurement for the activity.
C. Propose an experiment or improvement that can be worked on over the next 48 hours.
D. Spend 10-60 minutes working on the proposed experiment/improvement.
E. Meet every 48 hours to measure the current condition and document results of your experiment.
F. If current condition meets target condition for 3 meetings, select a new process area for improvement. if current condition does not meet target condition go to step D. and propose a new experiment.

6S Email Activity
0.5
Step 1: Sort
First things first, it’s time to get rid of all of the messages that are old and obsolete. Be aggressive
in this step. Don’t keep messages that you know will never be returned. If there are attachments
that need to be saved or printed, do so now. Get rid of as much as you can.
Step 2: Straighten
Now that what needs to be kept has been identified, the next task is to find a place for all of your e-mails. The easiest and most obvious way is to create folders based on tasks or other buckets of work where emails can be neatly filed for future use. The goal is to remove as much from your inbox as possible.
Step 3: Shine
Since we’re talking about computer applications, there really aren’t many necessary cleaning activities that would be required in a physical workspace. Still this is a good time to make sure your e-mail pages have the appropriate toolbars and proper layout. Clean-up anything that doesn’t look right.  Make sure you’re email signature and out-of-office reply are in an effective and professional format.
Step 4: Standardize
This step is critically important as you will now set rules for yourself to keep your e-mail account clean. Some rules you may consider are setting a maximum number of e-mails in your inbox. Once that number is exceeded, complete another sorting and straightening cycle. You can also set rules on how many emails are permissible in each folder. Another rule might be that any unopened e-mail will be automatically deleted after a certain number of days. The key is to set rules for yourself to keep things organized. If you are using Microsoft Outlook, you can manage your email rules using the Rules and Alerts function in the Tools menu.
Example Email Process Rules:
1. When number of inbox emails exceeds 20, identify:
a. Immediate Action
b. Planned Action/Keep Emails
i. Save to Task/Project Email Folder
ii. Archive
c. Delete Emails
2. When any email exceeds 90 days, identify:
a. Any action to be taken
b. Save to Task/Project Email Folder
c. Archive
d. Delete
3. Set Rules and Alerts in Microsoft Office
One of Outlook's best features is the capability to create and manage rules on how your email is handled. These rules can allow you to work with your email in any way that you like.
For example when you receive a message you can handle it based on criteria such as subject, sender, your name in the CC list.

Creating the rules is quite simple, as Microsoft has provided a wizard to do it and some templates to get you started.

Continuous Improvement
Sprint Planning
2.0
The Improvement Planning Meeting is time-boxed to 2 hours and consists of two segments that are time-boxed to 1 hour each.  The first segment is for selecting improvement backlog items; the second segment is for preparing the Improvement Sprint Backlog.
• Attendees are Continuous Improvement Team Member, the Process Owner, and the Team.  Additional parties can be invited by any of these people to provide additional business domain or construction domain information and advice, but they are dismissed after this information is provided.  No kibitzers are allowed as observers of Phase 1 Planning.
• The Process Owner must prepare the Improvement Backlog prior to the meeting.  In the absence of either the Process Owner or the Improvement Backlog, Continuous Improvement is required to construct an adequate Improvement Backlog that maps a look ahead of possible improvement work based on Failure Modes and Effect Analysis recommended actions and to stand in for the Process Owner.
• The goal of the Phase 1 Planning, or the first 1 hour, is for the Team to select those Improvement  Backlog items that it believes it can commit to turning into an implemented and fully documented improvement.  The Team will demonstrate this functionality to the Process Owner and stakeholders at the Improvement Sprint Review meeting at the end of the Improvement Sprint.
• The Team can make suggestions, but the decision of what Improvement Backlog can constitute the Improvement Sprint is the responsibility of the Process Owner.
• The Team is responsible for determining how much of the Improvement Backlog that the Process Owner wants worked on the Team will attempt to do during the Improvement Sprint.
• Time-boxing the first segment to 1 hour means that this is all of the time that is available for analyzing the Improvement Backlog.  Further analysis must be performed during the Improvement Sprint.  Large-grained, high-priority Improvement Backlog with imprecise estimates might not be thoroughly understood during this part of the Improvement Sprint planning meeting and might result in the Team not being able to complete all of the Improvement Backlog that it selects.
• The second segment of the Sprint Planning meeting (Phase 2) occurs immediately after the first segment and is also time-boxed to 1 hour.
• The Process Owner must be available to the Team during the second segment to answer questions that the Team might have about the Improvement Backlog.
• It is up to the Team, acting solely on its own and without any direction from outside the Team, to figure out during the second segment how it will turn the selected Improvement Backlog into turning into an implemented and fully documented improvement.  No one else is allowed to do anything but observe or answer questions seeking further information.
• The output of Phase 2 Planning is a list, called the Improvement Sprint Backlog, of tasks, task estimates, and assignments that will start the Team on the work of developing the improvements.  The task list might not be complete, but it must be complete enough to reflect mutual commitment on the part of all Team members and to carry them through the first part of the Improvement Sprint, while the Team devises more tasks in the Improvement Sprint Backlog.
 
 
 
 


Continuous Improvement
Daily Standup Meeting
4.0
The Daily Improvement Meeting is time-boxed to 15 minutes regardless of the number of Team Members.
• Hold the Daily Improvement Meeting in the same place at the same time every work day.  The Daily Improvement Meeting is best held first thing in the day so that the first thing Team members do on arriving at work is think of what they did the day before and what they plan to do today.
• All team members are required to attend.  If for some reason a Team member can’t attend in person, the absent member must either attend by telephone or by having another Team member report on the absent member’s status.
• Team members must be prompt.  Continuous Improvement starts the meeting at the appointed time, regardless of who is present.  Any members who are late pay $100 to Continuous Improvement immediately.
• Continuous Improvement begins the meeting by starting with the person immediately to his or her left and proceeding counterclockwise around the room until everyone has reported.
• Each Team member should respond to three questions only:
o What have you done since the last Daily Improvement Meeting regarding this project?
o What will you do between now and the next Daily Improvement Meeting regarding this project?
o What impedes you from performing your work as effectively as possible?
• Team members should not digress beyond answering these three questions into issues, designs, discussions of problems, or gossip.  Continuous Improvement is responsible for moving the reporting along briskly, from person to person.
• During the Daily Improvement Meeting, only one person talks at a time.  That person is the one who is reporting his or her status.  Everyone else listens.  There are no side conversations.
• When a Team member reports something that is of interest to other Team members or needs the assistance of other Team members, any Team member can immediately arrange for all interested parties to get together after the Daily Improvement Meeting to set something up.
• Non-Team members are not allowed to talk, make observations, make faces, or otherwise make their presence in the Daily improvement Meeting obtrusive.
• Non-Team members stand on the periphery of the Team so as not to interfere with the meeting.
• If too many Non-Team members attend the meeting, Continuous Improvement can limit attendance so that the meeting can remain orderly and focused.
• Non-Team members are not allowed to talk with Team members after the meeting for clarification or to provide advice and instructions.
• Any Non-Team members or Team members who cannot or will not conform to the above rules can be excluded from the meeting or removed from the Team.
 
 
 
 


Continuous Improvement
Sprint Review
2.0
The Improvement Sprint Review Meeting is time-boxed to 1 hour.
• The Team should not spend more than 15 minutes preparing for the Improvement Sprint Review.
• The purpose of the Improvement Sprint Review is for the Team to present to the Process Owner and stakeholders the recommended actions and improvements that were completed during the Improvement Sprint.
• Artifacts that aren’t actual components cannot be presented except when used in support of understanding the demonstrated improvement.  Artifacts cannot be shown as work products, and their use must be minimized to avoid confusing stakeholders or requiring them to understand how the systems are designed, detailed, fabricated and installed.
• Components should be presented by Team members.
• The Improvement Sprint Review starts with a Team member presenting the Improvement Sprint Goal, the Improvement Sprint Backlog committed to, and the Improvement Sprint Backlog completed.  Different Team members can then discuss what went well and what didn’t go so well in the Improvement Sprint.
• The majority of the Improvement Sprint Review is spent with Team members presenting their work, answering stakeholder questions regarding the presentation, and noting changes that may be required.
• At the end of the presentations, the stakeholders are polled, one by one, to get their impressions, any required changes, and the priority of those changes.
• The Process Owner discusses with the stakeholders and the Team potential rearrangement of the Improvement Backlog based on the feedback.
• Stakeholders are free to voice any comments, observations, or criticisms regarding the increment of work between presentations.
• Stakeholders can identify work that wasn’t delivered or wasn’t delivered as expected and request that such work be added to the Improvement Backlog for prioritization.
• Stakeholders can identify any new backlog items that occur to them as they view the presentation and request that the work be added to the Improvement Backlog for prioritization.
• Continuous Improvement should attempt to determine the number of people who expect to attend the Improvement Sprint Review meeting and set up the meeting to accommodate them.
• At the end of the Improvement Sprint Review, Continuous Improvement announces the place and date of the next Improvement Review to the Process Owner and all stakeholders.
 
 
 
 


Continuous Improvement
Sprint Retrospective
0.5
The Improvement Sprint Retrospective Meeting is time-boxed to 30 minutes.
• It is attended only by the Team, Continuous Improvement and the Process Owner.  The Process Owner is optional.
• Start the meeting by having all Team members answer two questions:
o What went well during the last Improvement Sprint?
o What could be improved in the next Improvement Sprint?
• Continuous Improvement writes down the Teams answers in summary form.
• The Team prioritizes in which order it wants to talk about the potential improvements.
• Continuous Improvement is not at this meeting to provide answers, but to facilitate the Team’s search for better ways for the Improvement process to work for it.
• Actionable items that can be added to the next Improvement Sprint should be devised as high-priority Non-Project Backlog.  Retrospectives that don’t result in change are sterile and frustrating.
